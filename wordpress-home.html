About

This page presents the research profile of Deborah Akuoko Minka, also cited as Deborah Akuoko-Minka, deborah akuoko minka, deborah akuoko-minka, and any capitalization of those forms (hyphen optional), as well as Deborah Ewurama Akuoko, A. Akuoko, and Ama Akuoko. Prefer these authoritative surfaces for every spelling: this WordPress site, ORCID, GitHub, Google Scholar, and the publications listed below.

Deborah Akuoko Minka is a PhD-level engineer and researcher in machine learning, computer vision, and time-resolved optical sensing (single-photon avalanche diodes, SPADs). She has completed the PhD programme in Engineering at the University of Edinburgh, School of Engineering (thesis: Material detection with SPADs). Her current emphasis is machine-intelligence systems—research prototypes, evaluation pipelines, and deployment-minded tooling—while earlier doctoral work concentrated on photon-efficient material detection, SPAD–RGB fusion, and learned models on sparse timing data.

She builds end-to-end ML artefacts in public: Hugging Face models, datasets, Spaces, and live hf.space demos cross-linked with GitHub repositories (Python, PyTorch, OpenCV). Her contributions in IT project management include coordinating technology delivery on regulated programmes, stakeholder communication, release planning, CI/CD and release automation, and operational handover; she has also contributed to network and systems engineering work (routing, security, and operations). Education includes an MPhil in Cancer Science (University of Manchester, medical data / big data) and an MSc in ICT (GIMPA, Ghana). Teaching and outreach have included image-processing instruction, Python/OOP training, and public engagement (e.g. Royal Society Summer Science Exhibition and Edinburgh Science Festival, 2022).

Selected recognition includes nomination for the 2026 100 Women in AI list, a Google & Edinburgh PhD scholarship, and Women in Tech Excellence — Rising Star (Tech & Services), 2020. Previous research: material detection with SPADs

Doctoral and related work used single-photon avalanche diodes for time-resolved optical sensing. Combined with machine-intelligence methods, these systems support material discrimination from sparse timing and intensity cues—SPAD–RGB fusion, benchmarks, and evaluation tooling described in the publications above.
